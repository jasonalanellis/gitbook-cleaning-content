# A Turnover Cleaning Checklist for San Diego Short-Term Rental Hosts

A guest's first five minutes in your rental decide most of your review. They notice the smell, the bathroom, the bed and the floor before they notice the view. A reliable turnover routine is how hosts keep those first five minutes the same every time, whether there are two days between bookings or two hours. This checklist covers what to clean, in what order, and what to check before you lock the door.

## Before you start: the turnover window

Work out your real window first. Checkout time to check-in time is the maximum, but laundry, restocking and a final walk-through all come out of it. Many hosts find the cleaning itself takes about half the window, and the rest goes to laundry, supplies and the unexpected, like a stain on the couch or a broken glass.

If your turnovers are tight, keep a second full set of linens and towels per bed and bathroom. Swapping in a clean set and laundering the used one afterwards takes the washer and dryer off the clock.

## Work top to bottom, dry to wet

The order matters. Dust falls, so clean high surfaces first and floors last. Do dry work, like dusting and vacuuming, before wet work, like mopping, so you aren't pushing dust into damp floors.

## Room by room

### Entry and living areas
- Pick up anything left behind and set it aside for the lost-and-found message to the guest
- Dust surfaces, shelves, TV stand and remotes
- Wipe light switches, door handles and the remote controls
- Vacuum sofa cushions and under them
- Straighten pillows and throws; wash throws if they've been used
- Check the patio or balcony: sweep, wipe the table, empty any ashtray if smoking is allowed outside

### Kitchen
- Run and empty the dishwasher; put everything back where your inventory says it goes
- Wipe counters, the stovetop, the front of appliances and the cabinet handles
- Clean the inside of the microwave
- Check the fridge and freezer: remove all guest food, wipe shelves and the door seals
- Empty the coffee maker, rinse the carafe and toss used filters or pods
- Take out trash and recycling, and replace the liners

### Bathrooms
- Clean the toilet inside and out, including the base and behind it
- Scrub the shower or tub, walls, glass and fixtures; clear the drain of hair
- Wipe the sink, faucet and mirror
- Restock toilet paper, hand soap and any toiletries you provide
- Replace bath mats and towels with fresh ones
- Mop last

### Bedrooms
- Strip every bed, even if it looks unused, and check the mattress protector for stains
- Make the beds with fresh linens, the same way every time, so the photos match what guests see
- Dust nightstands and lamps
- Check under the beds and in the closet and drawers for anything left behind
- Vacuum, including under the bed where you can reach

## The San Diego extras

Coastal rentals pick up sand everywhere: in the shower drain, in sliding door tracks, in the seams of the sofa. A handheld vacuum for door tracks and a quick check of the outdoor shower, if you have one, saves a lot of grit getting inside.

Salt air also leaves a haze on glass. Wiping the inside of balcony doors and windows each turnover, and the outside on a regular schedule, keeps the view looking like the listing photos.

If you rent in the City of San Diego, make sure your cleaning routine fits the rules for your short-term rental license tier, including how guests are told about trash and recycling days. The city publishes these rules for hosts.

## Common turnover mistakes

A few slip-ups show up in reviews again and again:

- Skipping the bed check because a bed looks unused. Guests sometimes nap on the spare bed or sit on it to pack. Strip every bed, every time.
- Forgetting the small touch points. Remotes, light switches, cabinet handles and the coffee maker are what guests touch first, and they notice when those feel sticky.
- Leaving a strong cleaning smell. A heavy scent can read as covering something up. Open windows while you work and let the place air out before you leave.
- Missing hair. One hair in the shower or on a pillow outweighs a spotless kitchen. Check drains, pillows and the bathroom floor last, with good light.
- Restocking at the last minute. Keep a sealed supply bin on site so a turnover never stops for a run to the store.
- Not checking the outside. A full trash can by the door or a dirty patio chair is the first thing a guest sees on arrival.

## The final walk-through

Before you leave, walk in the front door as if you were the guest:
- Does it smell clean, with no strong product smell?
- Is the temperature set to something comfortable?
- Are the Wi-Fi details, house manual and checkout instructions where they should be?
- Do all the lights work? Replace any dead bulbs now.
- Take photos of each room. They protect you if a guest later reports a problem, and they help you train anyone else who cleans for you.

## Keep a damage and supplies log

Note anything broken, stained or missing during each turnover, with the date and photos. Platforms usually set a deadline for damage claims, so a dated log made at checkout makes a claim far easier. A simple running list of supplies used each turnover also tells you when to reorder before you run out mid-booking.

## Doing it yourself or hiring it out

Many hosts clean their first few turnovers themselves, which is a good way to learn how long it really takes and to write a checklist that fits the property. Once bookings are steady, or you're managing more than one unit, a cleaning team that does turnovers regularly can take this off your plate. Look for one that will follow your checklist, send photos when they finish, and report damage and low supplies.
