# Weekly, Biweekly or Monthly? How to Pick a House Cleaning Schedule for a San Diego Home

Most people who hire a cleaner start with one question: how often? The honest answer is that the right schedule depends less on how big your home is and more on how it gets used. A two-bedroom condo with a dog and two people working from home gets dirtier, faster, than a four-bedroom house where everyone is out all day. This guide walks through how to choose, what changes between schedules, and how to tell when yours isn't working.

## Start with how your home actually gets dirty

Before picking a frequency, spend one normal week noticing where mess builds up. Write down the answer to three questions:

1. Which rooms do you use every day? Kitchens and bathrooms are almost always on the list. A home office or a kid's playroom may be too.
2. What tracks in from outside? San Diego homes near the coast pick up sand and salt air on floors, sills and sliding door tracks. Inland homes in places like El Cajon or Escondido tend to see more fine dust, especially in the dry months.
3. Who lives there? Pets, toddlers, roommates and frequent guests all speed things up. So does cooking at home most nights.

If the honest answers are "most rooms," "a lot" and "a full house," you're probably a weekly or biweekly home. If it's one or two people who are out most days, monthly with your own light upkeep in between can be enough.

## What each schedule is good for

### Weekly

Weekly cleaning keeps a busy home from ever getting to the "where do we even start" point. Floors, bathrooms and kitchen surfaces never build up, so each visit is mostly upkeep rather than catch-up.

It suits:
- Families with young kids or more than one pet
- People who cook at home most nights
- Homes that host often, or anyone who wants the house guest-ready all the time
- Anyone with allergies who wants dust and pet hair kept down consistently

### Every two weeks (biweekly)

Biweekly is the most common choice, and for good reason. It's frequent enough that bathrooms and kitchens don't get grimy, but it leaves room for you to handle the small daily things yourself: dishes, a quick wipe of the counters, picking up clutter.

It suits:
- Couples and small households
- Homes with one pet
- People who do a little tidying between visits and want the deeper work handled

### Monthly

Monthly cleaning works best as a reset rather than ongoing upkeep. Expect each visit to take longer than a weekly or biweekly one, because there's more build-up to work through.

It suits:
- One-person households, or homes where people travel a lot
- People who keep up with daily cleaning themselves and want the jobs they never get to, like baseboards, inside the microwave and behind the toilet
- Vacation homes or a second place that isn't lived in full time

## A simple way to decide

If you're stuck between two options, pick the more frequent one for the first two months, then step down if it feels like too much. It's easier to see whether you can stretch the gap once the home starts from a clean baseline. Starting with monthly visits on a home that hasn't had a deep clean in a while usually means every visit feels like catch-up.

A first visit that's a deeper clean, followed by a recurring schedule, is the pattern most cleaning companies recommend for exactly this reason. It sets the baseline so the regular visits can stay quick and consistent.

## Signs your schedule isn't working

Your schedule is probably too far apart if:
- Bathrooms look noticeably dirty again a few days before the next visit
- You're doing a "pre-clean" before the cleaner arrives
- Floors feel gritty underfoot by the end of the first week

It may be closer than you need if:
- Each visit finds very little to do in some rooms
- You'd rather put the money toward a periodic deep clean of the things that don't get touched, like windows, inside cabinets or the oven

## What to ask any cleaning company before you book a recurring plan

Whoever you hire, a few questions save a lot of frustration later:

- Will the same person or team come each time? Consistency matters more for recurring cleaning than almost anything else, because they learn your home.
- What's on the standard checklist, and what counts as an add-on? Inside the fridge, inside the oven and interior windows are commonly extra.
- How do you handle a missed spot? A good company will come back or fix it on the next visit without an argument.
- What's the policy for rescheduling or skipping a visit? Life happens, and you want to know before it does.
- Are the cleaners employees or contractors, and are they insured? Ask to see the proof.
- Can you change frequency later without a penalty?

## Making recurring visits easier

A few habits make any schedule go further:

- Clear counters and floors the night before a visit, so the time goes to cleaning, not tidying.
- Keep a short list on the fridge of anything you want extra attention on this time.
- Wipe the shower walls after use with a squeegee. It takes ten seconds and cuts soap scum build-up between visits.
- Shake out entry mats weekly. In coastal neighborhoods, most of the sand in a home comes in on shoes.

## The short version

Weekly fits busy, full homes. Biweekly fits most couples and small households. Monthly fits light-use homes and people who keep up with daily cleaning themselves. Start a little more often than you think you need, then adjust.
