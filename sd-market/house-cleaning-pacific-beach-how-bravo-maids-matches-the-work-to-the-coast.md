## What beach life does to a Pacific Beach home

Here is what I see in coastal homes, in plain terms.

Sand gets tracked in at the front door and spreads from there. It sits along baseboards, in rug fibers, and in sliding door tracks. Sand is basically tiny sandpaper, so the longer it sits on a floor, the more it scuffs.

Salt air leaves a light film. You see it first on windows, glass doors, and anything shiny on the ocean side of the house. It also collects on ceiling fans and the tops of cabinets, where nobody looks until the light hits it just right.

Wet towels and swimsuits live in the bathroom and laundry area after a beach day. That extra moisture means grout and shower corners need more attention than they would in a drier spot inland.

None of this means your home is dirty. It means your home is being used, which is the whole point of living ten minutes from the water.

## What a good cleaning should cover here

When our cleaners walk into a Pacific Beach home, the checklist follows the beach. Here is what that looks like.

Entry and floors come first. We vacuum before we wipe, so the sand gets picked up and not pushed around. Rugs, baseboards, and slider tracks get real attention, because that is where the grit hides.

Glass and window sills are next. Salt film comes off glass, mirrors, and sills so the light actually gets in. You moved near the ocean to see it.

Kitchens and bathrooms get their usual full pass. Our cleaners use steam on kitchen and bathroom fixtures every visit, and surface-appropriate cleaning products for the rest of the room. Grout lines and shower corners get extra time in homes that see a lot of wet gear.

High spots like fan blades, the tops of door frames, and the top of the fridge get dusted, because salt film and dust like to team up up there.

## How often should a beach household get cleaned?

This is the question I get most. My honest answer: it depends on how much beach your house sees.

If you have kids, a dog, or a standing date with the boardwalk, weekly or every two weeks keeps the sand from building up. If it's a quieter household, monthly can work fine. A lot of people start with a Deep Cleaning to reset the house, then move to Recurring House Cleaning to keep it there.

My nephews would vote for daily. They are not paying.

## Three things that help between visits

You don't need a new routine. Just three small habits.

Put a rough mat outside the door and a soft one inside. Two mats catch a surprising amount of sand.

Keep a bucket of water or a hose by the door for feet and toys. My nephews call this the car wash. They are fans.

Shake out towels and beach bags outside, not on the living room rug. I say this as a man who has learned it the hard way more than once.
